# Look

Follows cornercase-gpui's `design/widgets.md` and `src/theme.rs`: usecornercase.dev's colours, glass on one ground, rounded shapes with air, cobalt, the mark, motion. The tokens are ported from `theme.rs` into CSS custom properties; what changes for touch and small screens is recorded here. gpui's sources were read on `main` on 2026-11-08.

## Tokens

- **`src/lib/theme/tokens.css` keeps `theme.rs`'s names in kebab-case** (`brand_light` is `--brand-light`, `radius::CARD` is `--radius-card`), so a colour is found by the same name in both repositories. The colours are `mod colours`; `--glass` is gpui's opaque ground, the one it uses on Linux.
- **The whole palette is ported, not only what the first screen uses.** The states, `--veil`, `--field`, `--selection` and the rest are one piece of data copied as a whole, so the two repositories cannot drift apart; that is not dead code, which is about functions and components. A test checks that every `var(--x)` in `src/` is defined, in `tokens.css` or in the same file (a component's own property, like `Mark`'s `--unit`); it does not check that every token is used.
- **Spacing, fonts and durations are not in `theme.rs`, so their names are ours.** `--gap` (8 px) and the panel margins `--inset` (12 px) and `--inset-wide` (16 px) come from the literals in gpui's `ui/mod.rs`; `--inset-card` (28 px) is a card's own padding, shared by `Emblem`'s page and the actions under it so that the two keep the card symmetric; `--enter`, `--leave` and `--pop` from `ui/motion.rs`'s `ENTER`, `LEAVE` and `POP`. The diff's tints are ours too, since `theme.rs` has none: `--diff-added` and `--diff-removed` are `--done` and `--failed` at 10 %, their `-strong` forms, for the words that changed, at 32 % (`design/changes-and-files.md` › The screens). The durations also live in `src/lib/ui/motion.ts`, which the transitions read, and a test keeps the two equal.
- **No Tokyo Night colour** in the app's own look: Tokyo Night is the terminal's palette only, kept with the terminal (`src/lib/terminal/style.ts`, Terminal › Colours and styles).
- Colours with an alpha are written `rgb(r g b / a)` from the token's channels, not `color-mix()`, which iOS 15 lacks (the deployment target is 15.0).

## Ground and glass

- **The ground is opaque.** In gpui the glass shows the desktop behind the window; behind the WebView there is nothing, so the page paints gpui's Linux ground, `--glass`, with `glass::wash` at the top: `--brand` at 12 % fading to nothing over the top 180 px. The wash is on a `position: fixed` layer, so it does not scroll away and the rubber band of iOS scrolling never shows a light gap.
- **Floating cards keep gpui's values**: the vertical gradient from `#1c1f24` at 88 % to `#101215` at 91 %, the 1 px border, the `lift` shadow and the highlight along the top. Unlike gpui, WebKit does blur the page's own content under a `backdrop-filter`, so `blur(24px)` is set with and without the `-webkit-` prefix (iOS 15–17 only read the prefixed one). On the empty screen only the ground and the wash sit behind the card, so the blur barely shows; it will matter once content scrolls under a card. Blur costs GPU on large scrolling surfaces, so it goes on floating cards only, never on lists or the ground.
- **Checked by eye in the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.2, on 2026-11-08**: the card stands out from the ground at gpui's alphas, so none changed. The card's title is 20 px semibold, its lines 16 px with a line height of 1.4, the wordmark 22 px and the button's label 16 px medium. gpui sets 12–13 px text and a 17 px title for a mouse and a desktop; on a phone, body text goes to 15–17 px and titles to 20–22 px. The lines use `text-wrap: pretty`, which kept a single word from ending a paragraph; older WebKit ignores it. The mark and the card sit at the top of the screen rather than its middle, as the screens with machines will.

## Touch and motion

- **Every control is at least 44 px tall**, Apple's minimum touch target, and the pressed state is `:active`, never hover, which touch screens lack.
- **WebKit on iOS applies `:active` only to elements with a touch listener**, so `Button` and each toast carry an empty `ontouchstart`. Checked with a real touch on 2026-11-09 in the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.2 (an XCUITest press, which goes through the simulator's touch events like a finger): while `Add machine` is held it is `--brand-hover` (`#0050d8` read in the screenshots), and it goes back on release. Held for 10 s it does not click, since WebKit takes it as a long press; a tap opens the form.
- **Three tones, `primary`, `plain` and `danger`**: primary is cobalt, plain is gpui's `filled` of `Tone::Plain` (a `--hover` fill, a `--border` edge and `--soft` text), pressed to `--selected` with an `--edge` border. A form pairs them: `Cancel` plain, the action primary. Danger came with its first use, deleting a machine (027): `--failed` text on `--failed` at 10 % with an edge at 35 %, pressed to 18 % and 70 % (`color-mix`, so it follows the token). It marks only what cannot be undone: `Delete machine` and its confirmation's `Delete`, and the chat's `Ctrl-C` once armed, whose next tap interrupts the agent (Chat › Keys).
- **A resting button** keeps the primary fill at 40 % opacity and is `disabled`, `aria-disabled` and deaf to touches, like gpui's `resting_button`.
- **A busy button** is gpui's `busy_button`: the spinner before a label that says what is going on (`Adding…`), `disabled`, `aria-busy` and deaf to touches, but at full opacity, since it is working and not unavailable. With Reduce Motion the spinner stands still and the label alone says that it works.
- **The spinner** (`Spinner.svelte`) is Lucide's `loader-circle` at 12 px turning once every 900 ms in CSS, gpui's `spin`. It is mounted only while something works, so it never turns at rest.
- **Motion follows `ui/motion.rs`**: entering rises 14 px and fades in over 180 ms with `cubicOut` (gpui's `ease_out`), leaving fades out over 120 ms with `quadIn` (its `ease_in`), both from `svelte/transition` and `svelte/easing`. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, every duration is 0. Checked on 2026-11-08 in the same simulator with Reduce Motion on and off, on a recording of the launch read frame by frame: with motion, the card passes through intermediate positions and brightness; with Reduce Motion, it appears in its final place in one frame. Nothing animates forever while idle.
- **`dropIn` enters from the top**: `riseIn` with the 14 px negated, for the toasts, which come down from the top edge; they leave with `fadeOut`.
- **The pulse is gpui's `motion::pulse`**: opacity from 1 to 0.3 and back over 1,400 ms (`--pulse`, which a test keeps equal to `PULSE_MS`), on the connection bar's dot and the cursor of the mark on `home`, only while connecting (Connection › The connection bar). It is a CSS animation, not a Svelte transition, since it loops, defined once in `base.css` as the global `.pulsing` class, which each component sets; with Reduce Motion it does not run. It and the working orbit (Components) are the only animations that last, and each ends with its state.
- **`.spoken` hides text from the eye and not from VoiceOver**, such as the board's `h1` and the connection bar's `Retrying` without its countdown: one global class in `base.css`, like `.pulsing`, rather than a copy in each component's style.

## Fonts

- **System fonts only**: `--font-ui` is `system-ui` (SF Pro on iOS) and `--font-mono` is `ui-monospace` (SF Mono). The website's Archivo and JetBrains Mono were rejected for now: they add weight to the bundle, bring their licences into the app, and the CSP allows only `'self'`, so they would have to be bundled rather than fetched.

## Icons

- **Lucide is copied, not installed**, as gpui does in `assets/icons/`: version 0.460.0, with its ISC `LICENSE` next to the shapes in `src/lib/ui/icons/`. A package would bring in thousands of icons for the few the app draws.
- **The SVG's shapes are a table in `icons/shapes.ts`**, drawn by `Icon.svelte` with `stroke="currentColor"` and a stroke width of 1.75. Rejected: the `.svg` file pasted into a component (its `<!-- @license -->` line is a markup comment, which `scripts/check-rules.mjs` refuses), imported raw into `{@html}` (an error under `eslint-plugin-svelte`'s recommended rules), an `<img>` (it does not inherit `currentColor`) and a CSS `-webkit-mask-image` (it depends on the prefix on iOS 15 and goes through the CSP's `img-src`). An icon is added to the table, with its shapes copied from the same Lucide version, when a screen first needs it.

## Navigation

- **A router of our own, the route as state** (`src/lib/router.svelte.ts`): `Route` is a union tagged by `name` (`no-machines`, `home`, `agent` with its `machine` and `pane`, `terminal` with its `machine` and the agent's row, `machines`, `machine` with its `id` and `adding`, `start` with its `machine`, `changes` with its `machine`, `workspace` and `title`, and `change` with its `machine`, `workspace` and `path`), kept in a `$state` stack with `go`, `replace`, `setBase` and `back`, which never pops the first route. `replace` puts a route in the top one's place, never the base's: the agent the start screen started takes that screen's place, so `Back` goes to the board and not to a form already sent, and its `agent` route carries `starting` (`design/starting-work.md` › After it starts). That first route, the base, follows from the connection's state (`routeFor`): with no machine at all it is `no-machines`, otherwise `home`, which shows the board. A row of the board pushes `agent` with `go`, the agent's `Terminal` pushes `terminal` over it, its `Changes` pushes `changes` and a file of the list pushes `change` over that (`design/changes-and-files.md` › The screens), and each `Back` pops one. `changes` and `change` keep their machine for the same reason as `agent`, since workspace ids are each server's own too. The route keeps the machine it was opened on, since pane ids are each server's own: the connection bar also opens Machines over an agent, whose `Chat` lives on beneath it (`Router.opened`), and after switching machines there, `Back` comes back to that `agent`, whose `Chat` (made for the route's machine, `chatOf`) then shows it as gone rather than finding the new machine's agent with the same id (`design/chat.md` › The screen). A `terminal` keeps its machine for the same reason, and `terminal_open` opens its tab on the active machine: `openedTerminal` gives it only while that machine is still the active one, and otherwise `App` shows the gone agent in its place, with the connection bar, rather than opening that tab id on the new machine. The terminal's screen has no connection bar, so nothing opens Machines over it today; the rule covers any later way of switching under it. Rejected: dropping the `agent` routes from the stack on a switch, a second rule for what stays above the base besides `setBase`'s.
- **The connection sets only the base** (`setBase`): `App.svelte` calls it on each `connection-changed` and `agents-changed`, and what was pushed above the base stays. So the agent's screen and its terminal stay while the rows keep changing under them, every half second while an agent works, and Machines and a machine's screen stay open too, also when the active machine is removed (`connection-changed` to `none`) while others remain. A base of the same name leaves the stack alone. `no-machines` empties it, since nothing above it has a machine to show, so a terminal never outlives the last machine. A hot reload, which mounts `App` again, starts from the base. Rejected: replacing the top route on every change, as 014 did, which took the agent's screen back to the board and closed Machines at the next change; and 015's `settle`, which replaced the whole stack when the base changed, and so closed Machines once the first machine was added from it.
- **Ways in and back**: the connection bar's last control is a 44 px button with the `server` icon that pushes `machines` (P1). Rejected: tapping the bar's text, which does not look like a button, and a button on `home`, which belongs to the agents board. The board's `Start`, at its top right, pushes `start`, and rests while the connection is not `connected`, since nothing could be sent. A row's `Edit` pushes `machine`; `Scan again` pushes it with `adding`, which opens its `Add route` at once. Each of these screens goes back by its bar's back button or by the edge swipe (below). Deleting a machine pops back to Machines, then sets the base from the machines left. Android's back button (032) will call the same `goBack`.
- **The back button** is `chevron-left` and the title of the route below the top (`titleOf(router.below, …)` in `src/lib/navigation.ts`, pure: `Agents` for `home`, `Machines`, the changes' title, the agent's label, a machine's name, `Start`, a file's name), in `--accent` at 17 px, as Mail's `‹ Inboxes`.   - **`--accent` and not `--brand`**, which the ticket named: `#005fff` on `--veil` and `--glass` is about 3.8:1, enough for an icon (3:1) but not for 17 px text (AA asks 4.5:1). `--accent` is what the chevrons and the chat's bar already used.
- **One `goBack`** in `App.svelte` for the chevron and the swipe: it pops the top route, and closes the diff first when the top is a `change`, as `leaveDiff` did, so the two ways back cannot differ.
- **The edge swipe** (iOS only): a finger that starts within 20 px of the screen's left edge and moves right drags the screen with it, and lifted far enough it goes back. `BackSwipe` (`src/lib/backSwipe.ts`) is pure, with the clock passed in, like `swipe.ts` and `pinch.ts`; `watchBackSwipe` turns the touches into its calls, installed by one `$effect` in `App` only while `backSwipes` holds (iOS and a route below the top: never on the board or `no-machines`). Each new touch is ignored while `html.scanning` shows the camera.
  - **The thresholds**, close to UIKit's and to the terminal's 10 px tap (021): the swipe is claimed once the finger has moved 10 px, if more right than up or down, and dropped otherwise; lifted past 35 % of the width, or moving right at 0.5 px/ms or more, it goes back, else the screen settles. It is dropped when a second finger comes, on `touchcancel` or `pointercancel`, and when the finger stays more than 300 ms before it is claimed, which leaves a long press and text selection alone. A still tap at the edge stays a tap: the terminal's first column and the first key of the rows of keys still take it.
  - **Touch events, not pointer events**: only `preventDefault` on a `touchmove` that is not passive stops the terminal's native `pan-x` and the sideways scroll of the diff and the code blocks; once the browser pans, pointer events get only a `pointercancel`. The listeners are in capture on `document`, and `preventDefault` comes from the first move at the edge that goes more right than up or down (`holds`), while the swipe waits and once it is claimed: WebKit starts its pan within those first 10 px, and a `preventDefault` after it no longer stops it (Checked, below). A move from the edge up, down or left, and every touch off the edge, scroll the page as before.
  - **The shared edge** (`src/lib/edge.svelte.ts`, `idle`, `pending` or `claimed`), which the watcher writes and the terminal's grid reads: while the swipe waits or holds the finger, the grid ignores its moves (no lines to the PTY, no scrolling, no pinch), and a lift once claimed cancels the tap, so the keyboard does not open. Rejected: cutting the pointers with `stopPropagation` in capture, which would leave their ids hanging in the terminal's `Swipe` and `Pinch`. A touch that starts off the edge reaches everything as before: the pinch, `pan-x`, the swipe of 021 and the tap in the terminal, the sideways scroll of the diff and the code blocks, and the chat's scrolling and selection.
  - **`translateX` only during the swipe**: on `.page`, a wrapper of the screen without the connection bar and the toasts, and in the terminal on its fixed `.screen` (`shift`), since a transform on an ancestor turns its `position: fixed` children relative to it: the connection bar would jump on a scrolled page, and the terminal's screen would move with the page. At rest there is no transform at all. While it slides, `.sliding` paints `--glass` and a shadow along its left edge, over the app's ground and wash. `#app` clips sideways (`overflow-x: clip`, not `hidden`, which would make it a scroll container and unstick the bar), so the shifted screen never widens the page.
  - **Lifting**: back slides the screen out to the full width over `--leave` (120 ms, ease-out) and calls `goBack` once it is out; settling slides it back over the same time. The finger's own moves are not eased (`Shift`'s `eased`). New touches are ignored while it leaves. With Reduce Motion the screen does not move: past the threshold it goes back at once, and short of it nothing happens.

  - To check on an iPhone: the feel of the thresholds with a real finger, a drag up or down from the edge of a long chat (checked only on the terminal's grid), and Reduce Motion, unseen live.
- **Rejected for the swipe**:
  - Mounting the route below so that it shows while the screen slides: `App` mounts only the top route, and mounting the one below would start its streams (the terminal's `surface`, `changes list --follow`, the chat's reads); it would be cheap only for the board. The ground and the shadow show instead.
  - WKWebView's `allowsBackForwardNavigationGestures`: wry 0.57.1 has `with_back_forward_navigation_gestures`, but Tauri 2.12.5 does not expose it; it walks WebKit's back-forward list, of loads and `pushState`, while the router is state with no history; it shows an old snapshot of the screen before, not the live one; and it could not tell itself from the terminal's sideways scroll.
- Rejected: `svelte-spa-router` 5.1.1 and any URL or hash routing. A WebView has no address bar, so URLs give the user nothing, and they would bring a dependency and strings to parse; a union lets `pnpm check` check a route's parameters, such as the pane of 016's chat. `design/architecture.md` already chose routing as state over SvelteKit.

## Toasts

- **Where they come from**: the `toast` event, and the UI's own failures: a command's `CommandError` and a rejected IPC promise, which the bindings throw rather than return (`Toasts.failed`). The UI's own `info` toasts say what an action did where no screen shows it: a started agent's `warning`, and a workspace created (`design/starting-work.md`).
- **The logic is pure** (`src/lib/toasts.ts`, with the clock passed in), and `toasts.svelte.ts` holds the stack in `$state` with one timer set for the next toast to expire, rather than one per toast.
- **Adapted from gpui's `app/toasts.rs`**:
  - At most 3 rather than 5, the oldest dropped when a fourth comes: a phone has room for fewer.
  - At the top, under the connection bar and inside the safe area, rather than at the bottom right. On a phone the bottom belongs to the keyboard and the home indicator, and iOS's own banners come from the top. They enter downward (`dropIn`).
  - Each lasts 4 s (`info`) or 8 s (`error`) from when it arrives.
  - A toast equal to one still showing (same level and text) does not stack: it merges into it, counts `×N` and starts its time again, like `push_toast`. A `Store` repeats a warning, such as one per line of a stream that does not parse (Store › Lines are read leniently). A failed connection attempt is not a toast: its error is the connection bar's second line, so a stopped bridge shows none.
  - A tap dismisses it: there is no hover to show a close button on. A button fills the whole card, at least 44 px tall; it sits inside the `Glass` and not around it, since a button holds only phrasing content and `Glass` is a `div`.
- **Each one is a `Glass` card** with gpui's 3 px bar, `--accent` for info and `--failed` for error, with its glow. The stack lets touches through between the cards, so it never blocks the screen under it, and it is a polite live region.
- **Checked on 2026-11-09** in the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.2: three equal error toasts and a fourth, pushed by a temporary line in `App.svelte` through a hot reload (nothing in the app sends a toast on demand), showed as one `×3` card and the other, under the two-line bar of `retrying`. A tap on the first removed it and the second moved up.

## Forms

- **`Field`** (`src/lib/ui/Field.svelte`): its label above and its error's line under it (`Labelled`), a `--field` fill, an `--edge` border, `--radius-control` and at least 44 px. The text is 16 px: below that, iOS zooms the page when the field takes focus. On focus, an `--accent` border and gpui's `glass::ring`, 3 px of `--brand` at 32 % (`color-mix`, so it follows the token). The error's line keeps its height when empty, so an error does not move the buttons; with an error, the field is `aria-invalid` and described by it.
- **`TextArea`** (`src/lib/ui/TextArea.svelte`), beside `Field`, with the same fill, border, ring and 16 px, for the chat's answer: it starts at one line and grows with its text up to about six, then scrolls. It grows without script: a hidden copy of the text in the same grid cell sets its height (`data-value` and `::after`), so the height follows each keystroke without measuring. The copy clips what passes its maximum (`overflow: hidden`): hidden text still counts in the page's scroll area, and a long draft would hang below the field and lengthen the page with blank space. Return adds a line; the form's button sends (`design/chat.md` › Answering). In the chat its label is `aria-label`, since the chat shows no label above it; given an `error` (a form's field, such as the start screen's prompt), it takes `Field`'s face through `Labelled`: the label above, the error's line under it, `aria-invalid` and described by it.
- **A field turns text selection back on**: `base.css` sets `user-select: none` on `body`, and without `user-select: text` (and its `-webkit-` form) iOS neither selects in the field nor offers Paste on a long press, which is how the bridge's URL arrives.
- **A URL field** has no autocapitalisation, autocorrection or spell check, and `inputmode="url"`, so a pasted URL stays as it was pasted.
- **The first machine's form is inline**, in the empty screen's card under the emblem, like the Add form of gpui's `design/machines.md`: `Bridge URL`, `Name`, then `Cancel` and `Add`. Rejected: a sheet rising from the bottom. In WKWebView the keyboard covers what is `position: fixed` at the bottom (the visual viewport shrinks, the layout viewport does not), so the sheet would have to follow `visualViewport`. `Sheet` comes with its first real use: neither 027 nor 023 needed it. The start screen, which the ticket wanted as a sheet, is a screen of its own pushed over the board, with its own scroll, since its prompt always opens the keyboard (`design/starting-work.md` › The screen). What the form checks and does: Connection › Adding a machine.
- **Checked on 2026-11-09** in the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.2, with real touches: a tap on `Bridge URL` brings the URL keyboard (`/` and `.com`) and scrolls the field above it; a long press then offers Paste and AutoFill, and Paste puts in the bridge's whole URL, after which `Name`'s placeholder is its host. A long press on the field before it has focus offers nothing, as in any iOS text field. The simulator's pasteboard follows the Mac's, so `simctl pbcopy` is overwritten: put the URL on the Mac's. On `Add`, the spinner and `Adding…` show, the fields and `Cancel` rest, and the card fades out under the `Connecting…` bar.

## Machines

- **The list is one `Glass` card**, in the saved order, a `MachineRow` per machine: its name, then a `ToneDot` and the state's line in that state's colour.
  - The active machine's line is the bar's own text, its countdown included, from the same `retryClock`. The others show their last try (Connection › Machines).
  - A row that is not active is a button that connects to it, with `Check again` beside it (unless its token was rejected or it has none, when `Scan again` takes its place). While it is being checked, `Check again` rests and the spinner takes the dot's place.
  - The active row is `aria-current` and not a button. It shows `Scan again` while its token is rejected (Connection › The connection bar).
  - A row that is not active and whose probe says its token is rejected or missing shows `Scan again` in the place of `Check again`: the token does not change on its own, and adding the route already tries the machine again. So a row holds at most two buttons beside its name and state, as the other rows do; with three, the name and state had little room left at 390 pt (not yet looked at in the simulator). Rejected: wrapping the buttons onto a second line, which makes those rows taller than the others.
  - Every row has `Edit`. The errors of each route sit in a closed `details`, so a row stays short until it is opened.
- **`Add machine`** opens the same inline form as the empty screen (Forms) in a card under the list. `This device` closes the screen, with the fingerprint in `--font-mono`.
- **A machine's screen**:
  - The `Name` field and `Rename`, which rests until the name changes.
  - The routes, each with 44 px buttons to move it up or down and to remove it. They are disabled at either end and on the last route. A route can also carry `Connected` or `No token` in its state's colour.
  - Under them, `Add route` and `Delete machine`.
- **Deleting asks first**, inline, in a card that takes the place of those two buttons: the name, what is lost (its routes and their tokens on this phone, while the Mac and its bridge are not touched), then `Cancel` plain and `Delete` danger. Like the forms, it is part of the page rather than a dialog over it.

## Scanning

- **The camera shows through the page.** With `windowed: true` the plugin makes the WebView transparent and puts the camera behind it (Connection › Machines). The page still paints its opaque ground, though: `--glass` on `html, body` and the fixed wash of `body::before`.
  - `html.scanning` removes both and hides `#app`.
  - `ScanOverlay` attaches its layer to `body`, outside `#app`, so it stays visible: a square frame with gpui's `--veil` around it (a 100vmax shadow), a line that says what to point at, and `Cancel`.
  - The class goes on only once the camera is allowed, right before `scan`, and comes off in the scanner's `finally`, so a refusal or an error never leaves the page see-through.
- **A refusal or a failure** shows as a notice between `Scan QR code` and the URL field: `Open Settings` for a refusal, the plugin's message for an error. The field stays usable under it, for pasting.
- Not seen with a camera yet: the simulator has none, so the real scan is checked on an iPhone.

## Launch

- **No white flash and a light status bar.** `tauri.conf.json` gives the window `backgroundColor` `#0b0c0e`: wry 0.57.1 (`wkwebview/mod.rs`, read on 2026-11-08) paints it on iOS on the WKWebView and on its view with `setOpaque(false)`; without it the view uses `systemBackgroundColor`, which is pure black in dark mode and white in light mode. `LaunchScreen.storyboard` takes the same colour in sRGB instead of `systemBackgroundColor`, and `project.yml` sets `UIUserInterfaceStyle: Dark`, so the status bar is light whatever the phone's setting. Checked in the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 27.2, on 2026-11-08: the launch screen is the dark ground with a light status bar, and on a recording of the launch read frame by frame no frame between the home screen and the card is lighter than the ground.
- **`pnpm tauri ios dev` does not run xcodegen** once the project exists, so a change to `project.yml` reaches `cornercase-mobile_iOS/Info.plist` only by running `xcodegen generate --spec project.yml` in `src-tauri/gen/apple`; commit what follows from the change and drop the rest of the `.pbxproj` it rewrites. Each `ios dev` then writes `Info.plist` again without its final newline: that change is noise, revert it.

# The question

A topic of [Chat](../chat.md): the question read from the screen by a passive `surface`, the crop, the options, the fallback and the card, the composer's height, opening and closing it.

- **What it shows** (`src/routes/agent/Question.svelte`): while the agent is `waiting`, the composer holds, over the row of keys, the question (a menu of `AskUserQuestion`, a tool's permission, an approval), read from the rows of its screen: as a card laid out to the phone's width when the rows read as one (The card), else as those rows, drawn as the terminal draws them, with a button per numbered option between them and the keys (The fallback). Until the first rows come, and whenever there are none, the note of `waiting` shows instead (Chat › Answering). The row of keys stays as it was, under the options: `↑`, `↓` and `Enter` move and choose in the menu, and the card or the block shows the selection move.
- **Read from the screen, as upstream's Telegram does**: cornercase says that an agent waits (`status`, with `dialog: true` while a dialog shows) but not what it asks, and reading the agent's records is not the contract (Upstream gaps). Upstream's own Telegram bot answers the same question from the screen: the last 15 rows of the pane and a button per numbered option (`src/app/messenger/notices.rs`, which calls `messenger::choices` in `src/messenger/mod.rs`, #248). The chat does the same through the public `surface`, so it is no workaround, and the gap is named so that data can replace it.
- **A passive `surface` of its own** (`crates/core/src/terminal/asking.rs`, `Asking`): `surface --tab T --cols 80 --rows 24 --patches`, with no `view`, as the terminal's with its switch off (Terminal › Passive with the switch off): the terminal opens fitted since 043, the question's reader stays passive, so the machine's PTY keeps its size and `status` shows no `controller` for the phone. `--cols` and `--rows` are required but change nothing without a `view`: two captures of the same moment at 80×24 and at 40×60 had the same lines, with the pane at the PTY's size and all its rows, so the size is a constant and `question_open` takes only the tab and the pane. It runs over the terminal's `Live` and the core's reopening loop (`follow::reopen`, Changes and files › Following the list), with only the pane asked for: when it is not in the frame (closed, moved to another tab), the question is empty (`rows: []`), so the note comes back, never another pane's screen, which the WebView would drop while it kept showing the old question (review of 2026-11-10). Rejected: the terminal's choice (`Shown`, Terminal › The grid › One pane on the phone), which falls back to the active pane or the first. Rejected: cropping the rows the terminal's `Update` carries, which holds only the rows that changed, so the pane would be rebuilt again outside the grid; a `view` with the phone's size, which resizes the machine.
- **Its cost**: a second process on the machine and the tab's patches over the transport, only while the chat of a `waiting` agent shows; the crop runs on each change and the WebView hears only a question that differs from the last one, at most one `question-changed` per frame of 16 ms (`commands/chat/question.rs`, through the terminal's `by_frame`). As with the terminal, a passive `surface` never counts as seen, so the machine's notice goes on.
- **The crop** (`crates/core/src/terminal/question.rs`, `cropped`, pure and tested on Linux): drop the empty rows at the end; when the screen ends in options (`listed`, The options, its lowest within the last `QUESTION_ROWS`, 24, rows with text), start at the nearest rule above the first option that is not of `╌`, however far up; otherwise, from the last row with text, take at most 24 rows up and, within them, start at the highest rule; a rule being a row of only `─━═╌╭╮╰╯┌┐└┘` and spaces as wide as half the pane at least; drop the empty rows at the start. Why the options: a real `AskUserQuestion` of four options with descriptions of about 40 words is 30 rows from its rule to its help (`ask_long.json`), so a window of 24 lost its rule and its tabs, started at the rule inside the menu before `6. Chat about this` and showed only that option and the help, with no card and no buttons (review of 2026-11-10). The rule over the menu is the nearest above its first option, past the `╌` around a permission's command; the 24 rows still bound a screen without options, and options that end above them, such as a numbered list in the conversation, are not taken as a question. Rejected: a taller window, which only moves the limit and brings more of the conversation over a short question. Why: Claude Code 2.1.321 draws on the alternate screen with its content at the top and about half the rows empty under a menu; its menus start under a `─` rule as wide as the pane, and hold rules of their own (before `Chat about this`, `╌` around a permission's command), so the highest one in the window is the right start in every capture. The fixtures (`crates/core/tests/fixtures/control/surface/v1/question/`) are real lines of `surface` with Claude Code 2.1.321 and cornercase 0.13.19: one question, two questions in tabs, a Bash permission (16 rows each) and an agent at work (5 rows, its input box, no options); a folder's path was replaced by `/Users/dev/web-shop`. The trust question of a new folder is `idle` without a dialog and opens nothing.
- **Its limit**: the crop never shows history above the pane's screen, and a screen without options never more than its last 24 rows; and it is a heuristic over how each agent draws, so a new layout can show too much or too little until a capture adjusts it. The crop does not know whether the screen holds a question: it follows `waiting`, which comes after the menu is drawn and goes after it is gone, so for about a second after an answer the block may show the agent's input box instead (Checked live). Rejected: the last 15 rows as upstream's Telegram takes, which cut the menus of the captures (16 rows) or bring the conversation above a short one.
- **The options** (`options`, as `messenger::choices`): from the cropped rows, bottom up, a row that starts with `1.` to `9.` and a label, once the spaces and the marks `│┃|❯›>▶●○◯` are trimmed, is an option, up to the `1.` and without repeating a number; the rows that continue a long option are not options, so the button of an option that wraps holds its first row only (a permission's `Yes, and always allow access to`, its folder on the next rows). With fewer than two there are no buttons, so a lone numbered line of a message makes none. `Type something.` and `Chat about this` stay, as upstream keeps them. The card reads its options from the same walk (`listed`, which `options` wraps), so both agree on which rows are options.
- **The fallback's buttons**: one per option, 44 px high (Look › Touch and motion) and as wide as the composer, `N. label` cut with an ellipsis, named `Option N, label` for VoiceOver. A tap sends `keys --pane P N` through `Queue::ask` as the row's digits do (`Chat.pressOption`, Chat › Keys), with the same light tap and the same toast when it fails; a number that is not one digit sends nothing.
- **The fallback, drawn as the terminal draws it**: when the rows do not read as a card (The card › When it falls back), they go through the terminal's `Row.svelte` and `style.ts` (`runCss`, the pane's colours), in `--font-mono` at the terminal's 13 px, each row on its line without wrapping, as wide as the longest one with the cell measured as the terminal measures it (a hidden run of 100 `M`, iOS's monospace being wider than the 0.6 em that stands in until it is measured). The block is at most 40 % of the screen's height, scrolls both ways within itself (the page never scrolls sideways) and keeps its end in sight (`anchor` in `touch.ts`, as the terminal over the keys: Terminal › Typing), where the options and the selection are. Rejected: wrapping the rows to the phone's width, which breaks the menus' rules and columns; the card reads them instead.
- **The card** (`crates/core/src/terminal/question/card.rs`, `card(rows, cols)`, pure and tested on Linux; `src/routes/agent/QuestionCard.svelte` and `QuestionTabs.svelte`): from the cropped rows and their styles, the core reads the tabs of several questions (each label, whether it is answered, which one is open), the question as blocks of text and of code, the options (number, label, the detail under it, the chosen one) and at most two rows of help at the foot. It travels in `question-changed` as `card`, optional, next to `rows` and `options`, which stay for the fallback, and `Asking` sends a question again when only its card changed, as when the chosen option moves. The WebView only draws it: the text in `--font-ui`, wrapping; a command in `--font-mono` with `white-space: pre-wrap` and `overflow-wrap: anywhere`; each option a `Button` as wide as the card and at least 44 px high, with `N. label` and its detail under it in `--muted`, nothing cut with an ellipsis, the chosen one on `--selected` with a `❯` on the line of its `N. label` and `aria-current`, named `Option N, label, detail` for VoiceOver; the help small, in `--muted`, kept since `Tab to amend` says what nothing else does. So nothing of the question scrolls sideways on a 390 pt phone. A tap on an option sends its number as the fallback's buttons do. Why: the rows of a pane of 80 to 139 columns needed a sideways scroll to read a description, on the user's iPhone (029, 2026-11-10).
  - **How the rows are read**, as Claude Code 2.1.321 and 2.1.322 draw: the options are those of `options` (The options), each at the column of its digit, chosen when one of `❯›>▶` comes before the digit. The rows under an option, up to the next one, that are indented past its digit are its detail, joined with a space; blank rows and rules among them are skipped. Under the last option, the first row not indented past its digit starts the foot, whose rows that are neither blank nor rules are the help. Above the first option, the first row that is neither blank nor a rule is the tabs when its items, once `←` and `→` are dropped, each start with one of `☐✔☒✓` and carry a label, answered with `✔☒✓`, as Claude Code draws them right under its rule; only that row, so a line such as `✓ Tests pass` further down stays in the text (review of 2026-11-10). The other rows are text, a blank row or a rule ending a paragraph, and the rows between two rules of `╌` are a code block, with their common indent taken off and, when every row of it starts with `│`, that bar too (Claude Code 2.1.322 draws one down a Bash command). The rows of a paragraph are joined with a space when the row above was full (its next word would not have fitted in the pane's width) and with a line break otherwise, so a sentence that wrapped flows again and a list keeps its lines. Each row of a paragraph that starts with `│` loses that bar and its space, as Claude Code 2.1.322 draws one down a question of more than one row and down each question in the summary of `Review your answers`, whose `→` answers carry none, so neither the bar nor a line break shows in the middle of a question and VoiceOver does not read it; whether a row was full is still measured with its bar, which takes its columns, and the next word without it (`ask_long.json`, `ask_review.json`). A bar on every row or none, as in code, left the summary's bars in the middle of its sentences (review of 2026-11-11); code keeps that rule, since a `│` on some of a command's rows is the command's own. Rules of `─` are dropped.
  - **When it falls back** (`card` gives `None`): fewer than two options (an agent at work, its input box), a row under an option that is neither its detail, a blank row nor a rule, more than two rows of help, or a `╌` that never closes. The rows then show as before 042, so nothing is lost: a screen these rules do not know is read as the terminal draws it, never half of it as a card. Rejected: a card of whatever could be read, which would hide the rows it skipped.
  - **The open tab, by a background of its own**: the open tab is the one chip whose background differs from every other chip's of its row (153 in the captures), not a given colour, so another theme keeps it; with none, or more than one, no tab is known to be open (`open: null`). The chips wrap, so each shows whole: the open one with `--accent`, an answered one with its `✔` (U+FE0E after it, since iOS drew the plain `✔` as a grey emoji and not in `--done`). Each is at least 44 px high, the touch target of the rest of the app (`Button`). Their row stays at the top of the card as it scrolls (`position: sticky`, on the composer's `--glass`), ending in a band of `--gap` with a line of `--border` under it (a negative margin keeps the space under the chips at rest), so the row cut under them reads as cut and not as stray commas and strokes (review of 2026-11-11), and the card follows the chosen option below that row (`under` in `src/lib/reveal.ts`): once the card followed the chosen option of another tab, the chips were out of sight and another tab took a scroll up first (review of 2026-11-10). The `Submit` tab is named `answered Submit` to VoiceOver, since Claude Code draws it with a `✔` before anything is answered; kept as drawn (CA1). A tap on another chip sends the arrows that reach it, `right` × (target − open) or `left` × (open − target), in one `keys` call (`arrowsTo` in `src/lib/questionTabs.ts`, `Chat.openTab`), with a key's light tap and toast, so the arrows keep their order and nothing goes between them (Chat › Keys). The arrows count from the open tab of the last question heard, so after a tap the chips wait for the next `question-changed` before they send again, for the toast when the arrows did not go, or for 1.5 s (`TAB_HOLD_MS`, the question's frames of 16 ms with a wide margin) when the screen did not change, as with the focus in `Type something.`, where the arrows move Claude Code's cursor and no question comes: two taps before the screen changed sent the second from a stale tab and opened a third (review of 2026-11-10), and without the time the chips stayed dead for that question, with nothing said (review of 2026-11-11). The open chip, the chip of a lone question (a header) and every chip when no tab is known to be open are not buttons, since how many arrows to send is unknown. Accepted: with the focus in `Type something.`, the arrows move Claude Code's cursor instead of the tab; the card shows what happened. The `Submit` tab's screen, `Review your answers` with `1. Submit answers` and `2. Cancel`, reads as a card too (Checked live).
  - **It gives way and follows the chosen option**: the card is the part of the composer that gives way, as the block (`flex: 0 1 auto`, at most 40 % of the screen), down to the chips' band and three lines (`min-height: calc(44px + 2 * var(--gap) + 3lh)`), and scrolls up and down within itself (`overflow-x: hidden`, `overscroll-behavior: contain`). When the chosen option changes, and when the card changes height (`bind:clientHeight`: the keyboard opening, the row of keys), an `{@attach}` scrolls it by what brings that option into sight (`revealBy`, `src/lib/reveal.ts`, tested with vitest). When the question changes, another tab opened by a chip or by an answer, or a question of its own, the card first goes back to its top, so the question reads from its start, and then scrolls towards the chosen option if it is out of sight, but never past the question's first line, which stays under the chips (`scrolledTo`'s `lead`): when the question and the option do not fit together, the question wins and the option shows only in part, or not at all. In `Review your answers` the summary is the question, so after long questions `1. Submit answers` starts below it, a swipe away. That holds until `↑` or `↓` moves the choice within the question, or a swipe takes the question's start out of sight; then the card follows the option whole again, also as its height changes. A question is its open tab and its blocks, since the chosen option is often `1.` in the next one too. Why: following only the number, a tab opened with the card scrolled kept the scroll of the one before, its question starting mid-sentence or its chosen option under the chips, and the `Review your answers` summary hid `1. Submit answers` (review of 2026-11-11). Why the height too: with the keyboard and the keys open, the card shrank to about 40 pt and showed only its chips, the chosen option out of sight (Checked live). Why the question wins: it is what has to be read first, and with descriptions of about 40 words the question over three rows and the whole chosen option did not fit together in 40 % of the screen, so the question started a line under the chips on the first look, on a chip and on an answer (third review of 2026-11-11). Why the minimum: with the keyboard and the keys open the card shrank to 43 pt, under the 55 of its sticky chips, so neither the question nor an option showed and nothing could scroll them into sight (fifth review of 2026-11-12); the block keeps three rows for the same reason. Past that minimum the composer, capped under the bar (below), scrolls within itself: with the keyboard open the field sits under its edge until a letter is typed, when iOS scrolls it into sight and the chips go above. Rejected: dropping the chips' `sticky` on a short card, which leaves two lines of the question and the tabs out of reach. Why the `❯` sits on the label's line: centred in a button of about 170 pt, it fell under the card's edge when only the top of `1.` showed under the question (fourth review of 2026-11-11). The question's start counts as out of sight only a pixel or more above the chips: at rest it sits right at their edge, and a fractional scroll on a 3× screen must not stop the cap. Rejected: revealing only the option's first line on a new question, which still hides the question when that line does not fit under it either; `scrollIntoView`, which scrolls every box around the option, the composer and the page too.
  - **Detail or continuation**: the drawing does not tell a description from the rest of a label that wrapped, so a permission's `Yes, and always allow access to` shows its folder and `from this project` as a detail under it, in `--muted`, one sentence in two parts; a folder longer than the pane's width wraps inside it. Accepted until the question is data (Upstream gaps). A multi-select's `[ ]` and `[✔]` stay in its labels, not read.
  - **Rejected**: wrapping or splitting the rows in the WebView, which breaks the menus' rules and columns and still shows a description as rows of a terminal; reading the rows in TypeScript, when the crop, its fixtures and their tests are in the core and the WebView only draws (Code layout); a command of its own for the chips' arrows, when `keys` takes a list (Chat › Keys › `keys`, not `input`).
- **The composer never covers the bar or the title** (`src/routes/agent/Composer.svelte`, `.composer`): it is at most the screen's height minus the safe area, the connection bar (`--below`) and `--over-composer` (132 px: the chat's bar, 60 px, the header with the agent's name and state, about 44, and the gaps between them), and scrolls within itself past that. The block is the one part that gives way (`flex: 0 1 auto`), down to three rows, so the options, the keys and the field keep their size and the block scrolls instead. Why: with five buttons, two rows of keys and the field, the composer was taller than the space under the bar on a 390 × 844 phone, and as `position: sticky; bottom: 0` it grew upwards, under the bar, where nothing could scroll it back: a question in tabs lost its row `← ☐ Colour ☐ Size ✔ Submit →` and the header its title (review of 2026-11-10). A constant rather than a measure, since the bar and the header have fixed heights; a bigger text size would need it measured. While the question shows, the thread is under the composer; it comes back when the question goes. Rejected: two columns of buttons, which cut labels of any length at half the width; a row of buttons that scrolls sideways, which hides options.
- **Opened and closed with the screen** (`Chat.watchQuestion`, `src/lib/question.svelte.ts`): `Agent.svelte` watches the question while it is mounted and the page shows (`whileShown`), and the `Chat` opens it while its row is `waiting`, so another status, the agent gone, `Back`, another machine, the background and the terminal, the changes or Machines over the chat close it, and `waiting` again opens it again. It listens to `question-changed` before it opens, so it misses no event, and opens and closes one after the other, in the order asked; an event of another machine, tab or pane, or of an opening already closed, is dropped.
- **A slot of its own**: `question_open(tab, pane)` and `question_close(tab, pane)` keep the task in `Active`'s third slot (Architecture › Commands and events), so closing the question never stops the terminal of the same tab, nor the other way round. The slot is keyed by the tab and the pane: two agents in splits of one tab have a chat each, each with its own queue of opens and closes, so going from one to the other may open the new one before the old one closes; with the tab alone, that late close would end the new question (review of 2026-11-10).
- **No toast**: a `surface` that fails, a line that is not a surface or a lost transport empty the question (`question-changed` without rows), which brings the note back, and a tab that closed sends `closed`; neither is ever a toast. A line that is not a surface also forgets the last question sent, so the next frame sends it again even when the screen did not change, and a menu that stays still comes back (review of 2026-11-10). The question only adds to the keys, which still answer and tell their own failures, and the terminal tells the same failure when it is opened.
